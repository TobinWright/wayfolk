# Wayfolk: design so far

What the user has decided (2026-10-27). The user leads the design piece by piece: ask before deciding anything not written here.

## The game
- A social idle incremental MMO in the browser, 2D, one shared world.
- Players have several characters (heroes). Characters doing similar tasks group up automatically with other players' characters.
- Players make classic games (poker, checkers, chess...) to play each other, wagering gold, and tournaments for rare items.
- Sites are static: the same places with the same items, always. Change comes from dynamic events with goals.
- More player choice is wanted, carefully: too much may be bad.

## The map (built)
- `client/tools/render-world.js` (`npm run world` in client) builds it from `client/tools/reference/world-ref.png` and `client/tools/world-layout.ts`: 292 x 176 tiles (16 tiles of sea added over and under the reference, 50 either side of it), Apollo palette, animated water, place sprites. Forests, pines, hills and mountains are traced off the reference in world-layout.ts, with the swamp, the volcano's ash, rivers and places (the user asked for the traced layout back, 2026-10-27, over the tool working out where they go). They're drawn with the tileset's corner-set tiles (the user's pick, 2026-10-27, over painting them tree by tree and peak by peak - `npm run world -- --painted`, `client/src/art/WorldCover.ts`, still there to compare). The grass is five versions of one tile (tufts and flowers in other places) picked at random - the user's call, 2026-10-27, after patches of other grass looked wrong. See the comments in those files.
- **The map editor** (admin menu, Map; the user, 2026-10-31: "build me a robust map editor for the admin menu", "it should paint autotiles"): editors paint the map's tiles over the map itself - land and sea, snow, desert, swamp, ash, forest, pines, dead wood, hills, mountains, peaks and rivers - by brush (sized), fill or pick, with undo. Each tile is chosen from its corners as `render-world.js` chooses it (`client/src/art/WorldAutotile.ts`, shared by both, so the shores, shallows, deep water and rocky coasts come by themselves). The server keeps what's painted in `world.json` and, corner by corner, in `client/tools/world-paint.json`, which `npm run world` puts over what it works out, so a rebuild keeps it. Everyone online sees it painted. Places are put on the map, moved (dragged), renamed, given another kind or sprite (one no other place has), or taken off, with undo: the server changes `world.json`, the place's line in `world-layout.ts`'s PLACES (the comments between them kept) and its node (moved and renamed with it; a new one's made, linked to the nearest; one taken off stays, a waypoint), and the ground under a place is made as `render-world.js` makes it (land under it, a clearing round it), so a rebuild matches. Places left off the land are pointed out. Off on the live box (`MAP_EDITING=0`). See `shared/MapEdit.ts`.
- **Every place has a sprite of its own** (the user, 2026-10-27): a kind's drawing (`client/src/art/PlaceArt.ts`) for the first of it, and for the others one of 17 `PLACE_VARIANTS` drawn for where they stand (a snowy village, castle, farm and stave church on the snowy island; a domed chapel, nomad tent, sandstone cave and caravan camp in the desert; Rockbarrow among boulders, Luminmoor lamplit in the forest, Gullhaven by the sea, a palisade fort, a brick estate, an orchard, the Emberlet Mines glowing, the Woodcutters Camp with its log piles, overgrown ruins), named by `pic` in world-layout.ts. render-world.js stops if two places share one.
- **Moving it** (the user, 2026-10-28): dragging, the wheel to zoom, and WASD or the arrow keys to pan (`Map.PAN_SPEED`, 800 screen px a second; by key position, so WASD on any keyboard), not while the chat is typing.
- Place names in world-layout.ts are a first draft. **Heroes start from one of three towns** (the user, 2026-10-27): Rockbarrow, Luminmoor (right of the inland lake) and Gullhaven (the southernmost settlement, a manor; Claude's draft name).
- **The travel graph** (built, 2026-10-27; the user's picks: replace the old map's nodes, places plus waypoints, sea routes to the islands): `client/tools/build-nodes.js` (`npm run nodes` after `npm run world`; `-- --db` replaces the server's `node` table, then restart the server) puts a node on every place (its id the place's in world.json) and links neighbours by the cheapest way over land (forests, hills, swamp and fording rivers cost more, mountains nearly bar it), a waypoint about every 7 tiles, ways shared as roads; the desert and snowy islands by sea from the nearest mainland shore (Glassmere Quay is on the inland lake, linked only to Glassmere). Levels are all 1 for now. The node editor (admin menu, Nodes; rebuilt 2026-10-29) edits it by hand after: it draws the graph over the map; tools Select, Add (a node linked to the one picked, or put in a link), Link and Remove (keys 1-4); drag a node to move it (to a tile's middle, Shift to the px); every change is sent at once and can be undone (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y); it lists the graph's problems (one-way links, nodes cut off or with no links, places with no node or one off them, nodes on top of each other). The server keeps links both ways, won't remove a place's node or one a hero is at or going through, and sends everyone the new graph after.

## Heroes (being designed)
- The first hire is free.
- Heroes differ by skills, stats, class/job and traits/quirks (all four).
- More heroes are hired for gold.
- Roster slots are capped, more unlocked over time.
- All heroes come the same way (the first is only free): picked from premade heroes on the hiring board, which refreshes every so often. No renaming or changing looks, ever.
- Up to 5 offers on a board (the exact number is still to settle; suggested 3).
- **Hiring is global** (the user, 2026-10-27; it was a board per town): one board, opened from the main menu (Hiring) wherever you are. **Each offer starts from one of the three start towns**, shown on its card.
- Each player has their own offers on the board (nobody takes a hero from anyone else).
- Boards refresh every 4 hours. **Or now, for gold** (the user, 2026-11-03: "a refresh button (for gold)"; the numbers are Claude's): Refresh on the board, left of the roster count, new offers (hired ones too) for 50g (`REROLL_PRICE`), doubling with each in the same 4 hours (`rerollPrice`; `hero_board.rerolls`), back to 50 with the next 4.
- Races: the fantasy races (the list below is Claude's proposal). **For now every hero is human** (2026-10-27): the other races wait until the people art can show them.
- Skills: a core set, and an extended set for more niche learning.
- Jobs and stats: the user left them to Claude, with balance in mind (below, open to change).
- A new player has **3 roster slots** and **starts with 0 gold** (2026-10-27).
- **A hire's price is by how well the hero rolled** (2026-10-27): about 50-300g; the first hire is free.
- Clicking a place on the map spun its menu icons out round it (a ring menu, 2026-10-27); since then (the user, same day) clicking a place did nothing and pointing at it shows its name. Now clicking a place opens it (the user, 2026-10-28: below, "A place's window").

## Heroes: Claude's proposal (jobs and stats were left to Claude; the rest is to confirm)

**The rule of balance:** what a hero has trained (skills) matters most; what they were born with (race, stats, job, traits) makes them about 20-40% better or worse at something. Enough that picking from a board matters, never so much that a hero is useless outside their speciality.

**How well a hero does an activity:** base x skill x stat x job x traits (x crew x gear, later).
- Skill: 1 + 0.03 per level over 1 (level 1: x1.0, 50: x2.5, 100: x4.0).
- Stat: 1 + 0.02 per point over or under 10 (5: x0.9, 15: x1.1, 20: x1.2).
- Job: its 3 favoured skills give x1.1 and 25% more XP.
- Traits: about 5-15% each, up or down.

**Stats (5)**, rolled on the board and fixed (they grow only with gear and traits, so a well-rolled hero is worth hiring):
- Strength: mining, woodcutting, smithing, hitting hard.
- Agility: hunting, fishing, scouting, travel speed, dodging.
- Intellect: alchemy, tailoring, woodworking, magic.
- Vitality: how long they work before resting, health.
- Luck: rare finds, critical hits, how events turn out.
Rolled around 10 (about 6-14 in Rockbarrow), higher in higher-level towns.

**Races (4)**: Human, Elf, Dwarf, Orc (the user cut halfling, gnome, goblin, lizardfolk, beastkin and troll). Each tilts the stats (the tilts add up to +2; humans have none but get an extra trait):
- Human: no tilt, +1 trait. Elf: Agi +2, Int +1, Vit -1. Dwarf: Vit +2, Str +1, Agi -1. Orc: Str +2, Vit +1, Int -1.

**Core skills (12)**, levels 1-100, every hero has all of them; one each for the sites' activities:
- Gathering: Mining, Woodcutting, Foraging, Fishing, Hunting.
- Crafting: Smithing, Woodworking, Alchemy, Cooking, Tailoring.
- Adventuring: Fighting, Scouting.
A hero on a board starts with 2-3 of them above level 1 (1-5 in Rockbarrow, more in higher towns).

**Extended skills** (the old list of 75 less the core ones: Seamanship, Lip Reading, Cartography, Diplomacy...): skills like the core ones (levels 1-100, the same XP curve), but no hero starts out knowing any (none rolled on the board). Any hero can learn any of them, at an academy (not built yet) or out in the wild, and from then on they train like the rest.

**Learning in the wild** (the user, 2026-10-29; Claude's table and numbers, open to change): each core skill leads to 2-4 related extended skills (`extFromWork` in `shared/Tasks.ts`: Fishing to Swimming, Seamanship, Navigation; Hunting to Tracking, Trapping, Taming, Anatomy...), and dungeon runs and adventures to a few of their own (Disarm Trap, Escape, Detection; Camping, Survival, Navigation, Folklore). Each unit of work, task, road fight or event (`HeroManager.learnAround`): the related ones a hero knows get 30% of its XP each (`EXT_XP_SHARE`, specialties counting), and they have its time at x1 over 4 hours (`EXT_LEARN_MS`) as a chance to pick up one they don't ("Picked up Tracking" in the journal): about one every 4 hours of related work at a new hero's pace. The away summary shows them too ("new" for one picked up). They don't speed up work: they open things - an event's choice, a site's special action (Seamanship for sea routes), contests at fairs.

**Specialties** (the user, 2026-10-29): each hero rolls 1-2 skills they're a natural at, core or extended (an even chance of each): 1.5x XP at them (`SPECIALTY_XP`, on top of a job's 25%). An extended one still has to be learned first. Heroes from before them were given some on loading (from their name, the same every time). Each adds 10g to a hire's price.

**Jobs (8)**, set on the board and fixed (the user: probably fixed); each a role in fights and 3 favoured skills:
- Warrior (tank): Fighting, Mining, Smithing.
- Ranger (ranged): Hunting, Scouting, Woodcutting.
- Rogue (striker): Scouting, Fishing, Tailoring.
- Mage (magic): Alchemy, Foraging, Tailoring.
- Cleric (healer): Fighting, Cooking, Foraging.
- Druid (nature): Foraging, Woodcutting, Fishing.
- Artisan (crafter): Smithing, Woodworking, Jewelcrafting (Tailoring until 2026-10-28).
- Bard (support): Cooking, Woodworking, Scouting.

**Traits** (the user, 2026-10-29: "a full game's worth"; Claude's list and numbers, built): 1-2 a hero (humans 2-3), good, bad or mixed, 59 of them in `TRAITS` (`shared/Heroes.ts`), each doing what it says through fields read where that thing's worked out (`traitMul`, `traitAdd`, `traitWork`). Two of a group never roll together (Night Owl / Night Blind / Early Bird / Nocturnal; Lucky / Unlucky...: `rollTraits`). Rare ones (Moon-Touched, Sun-Blessed, Night Stalker, Storm-Born, Treasure Hunter, Prodigy, Berserker) are in the running only 15% of the time on a level 1 board (about 6% of heroes get one), always on higher ones. What they touch:
- **Work**: all work (Hard Worker, Easily Distracted, Lazy, Restless), one skill each 15% (Green Thumb, Angler, Stonehand, Axe Arm, Keen Nose, Forge-Born, Whittler, Herbalist, Gourmet, Pathfinder; Steady Hands two at 10%), crafting (Butterfingers, Perfectionist - who now and then makes two), Thrifty (now and then no materials used), Clumsy (1 in 20 gathered or crafted fumbled: nothing made, half the XP), Strong Back (1 in 10 gathered brings another, more finds).
- **The time of day and the weather** (below): Night Owl, Early Bird, Night Blind, Nocturnal (work and travel), Moon-Touched and Sun-Blessed (XP), Night Stalker and Superstitious (fighting at night), Rain Lover and Fair-Weather, Storm-Born, Sea Legs and Seasick.
- **XP and gold**: Fast Learner, Slow Learner, Greedy, Haggler, Spendthrift, Curious (picks up extended skills twice as often), Prodigy (specialties twice as fast).
- **Stats**: Lucky, Unlucky, Brawny, Frail, Nimble, Hardy and Sickly (and healing), Bookworm.
- **Getting about**: Wanderer, Heavy-Footed, Restless, Trouble Magnet and Light-Footed (fights on the road), Treasure Hunter (finds).
- **Fighting**: Brave, Cowardly (dodges more), Berserker, Thick-Skinned, Glass Jaw, Sharp Eye (strikes true more).

**Hiring cost**: by the hero's roll and the town's level (Rockbarrow's first hire free; `heroPrice` in `shared/Heroes.ts`: 100g, +6 a stat point over 10, +5 a skill level, +10 a specialty, +-20 a good or bad trait, x1.5 a town level over 1, kept to 50-300 a level).

## Locations (the user, 2026-10-27)
- **Locations have levels.** A party goes only as deep as it can handle, so every location is usable by everyone: a new hero works a place's first level, a strong party its deep ones.
- **How deep: by party power** (the user's pick over the activity's skill, or both).
- **Some crafting materials are only found at or below a certain level of a location.**
- Built as rules both sides will use, `shared/Locations.ts` (the numbers and the materials are Claude's proposal, open to change; nothing uses them until activities, next step 4):
  - Levels by the kind of place (`LEVELS`): mines, dark towers and the volcano 15; caves, lairs, ruins, pyramids, the ice castle and woodcutters' camps 12; craters, step temples, the big tree and altars 10; standing stones, temples and towers 8; oases 6; anything else 5.
  - A level's danger: 10 x level^1.3 (10, 42 at 3, 149 at 8, 253 at 12). A hero's power: 10 x Fighting (1 + 0.03 a level over 1) x Strength, Agility and Vitality's average (1 + 0.02 a point over or under 10) x 1.1 if their job favours Fighting. A party's is its heroes' added up; it works the deepest level whose danger it meets (`deepestLevel`). One new hero: level 1; five new: 3; five at Fighting 50: 7; five masters: 11 (deeper will take gear).
  - 64 crafting materials (`MATERIALS`, saved ids; 48-64 the content pass's: "Potions and meals" below): each found at some kinds of place from a least level - ores in mines (Copper 1 ... Mithril 6, Adamantite 9), stone and gems in caves (Quartz 1, Amethyst 8, Deep Crystal 11), logs at woodcutters' camps (Pine 1 ... Ironwood 12; levels moved for the tiers: "Equipment"), hides, fangs and scales from lairs, sulfur to magma cores at the volcano, starmetal in craters, frost crystals at the ice castle, runestones and relics in ruins, shadow essence and void shards in dark towers, flax, wool and silk on farms.

## Hero windows (the user's design)
- Every hero you have gets their own window, 10 x 3 tiles: a side-view platformer scene of their group (a party of up to 4 or 5).
- The group walks right or left by the way they're heading, and the background changes to match where they are.
- Battles happen in it: enemies show up in the window.
- Characters and cosmetics use Lithalia's sprites.
- Two or more of your heroes in the same party share one window.
- Where they sit: **always over the party's marker on the map** (the user, 2026-10-28; it was a column at the top right or over the marker, switchable). The top right now has the roster (below). Zoomed right out (0.5) the window gives way to its party's names, stacked in a small box with a point to the marker; heroes on the map are drawn at half size (the user, 2026-10-28).
- The end goal: the window shows what they're doing (mining, fishing, chopping... not just walking and fighting).
- Battles are only watched; there's nothing to do in them.

## Moments on the road (the user, 2026-10-30: "small choices that come up during play... traits, stats and extended skills open extra options. If you're signed out, a default is picked and the result goes into the journal and the away summary"; built. The moments, odds and numbers are Claude's, open to change)
- **The rules** are `shared/Choices.ts`. 17 moments (`MOMENTS`, saved by id, their ways by place): 10 on adventures (The Rope Bridge, The Trapped Merchant, The Flooded Ford, Fresh Tracks, The Wayside Shrine, The Wounded Traveller, Bandits!, Lost in the Fog, Dicing Soldiers, Night Falls - at night only) and 7 on dungeon runs (The Locked Chest, The Sleeping Beast, The Loose Flagstone, The Groaning Ceiling, The Fork, Whispers, The Fallen Adventurer). Each has 3-6 ways through: tested by a stat, a core skill or both, or by an **extended skill - shown only to heroes who know it** (that's what they open; 30 of the 63 are used), or open only to a trait (Bandits: Brave or Berserker "Stare them down"; the chest: Treasure Hunter "Find the false bottom", sure), and one sure way (go round +20 min, pay the toll, walk on...).
- **How often**: a unit's time at x1 over an hour (`MOMENT_MS`): a 1 in 6 chance a place looked about on an adventure, 1 in 3 a dungeon run won; about one an hour of either. One at a time a hero.
- **The odds** (`chance`): 45%; 3% a point of the stat over or under 10 (with jewellery), half a percent a level of the core skill; knowing the extended skill +20% and half a percent a level; a trait's own way +15%; a point of Luck over 10 +1%; 5-95%. Shown on each way.
- **What comes of it** (each way's well and badly, `Outcome`): gold (so many dungeon runs' worth at the place's level; lost too, never more than they have), finds (materials of the place at that level), hurt (resting where they are if it's all their health) or rest (healing), time held up (`HeroState.y`: they wait that long before getting on: "Held up on the road (N min)"). XP into the skill tested (10 minutes' worth at x1, half if it went badly), and a way with `learns` (most stat ways: crossing the bridge Balancing, bargaining Diplomacy, joining the dice Gambling...) a 1 in 5 chance of **picking up that extended skill** if it went well (Curious twice): the way to the ones no work leads to.
- **Signed in**: the moment waits on you (`HeroState.c`, sent with their state) for 90s (`CHOICE_MS`), the hero working on meanwhile. **The window** (`components/herochoice/HeroChoice.ts`, opened as one comes, one at a time): "<name>: <moment>", what's happening, a row a way open to them (its number and name, the chance - green, amber, red; Sure -, what's tested on the left and what comes of it on the right, well in green / badly in red), their own pick ringed, a bar running down ("They pick in 74s") and Let them pick. A way clicked (or its number typed) is the answer (`C2S.HERO_CHOICE`); Escape or the cross leaves it to them now. What came of it shows after (`S2C.HERO_CHOSE`: It went well / badly and the journal's line) till Continue (Enter), then the next one waiting.
- **Signed out** (or out of time, or one left waiting as they left): **they pick for themselves** (`ownPick`): the likeliest tested way if it's 60% or better (Brave and Berserker 50%, Cowardly 75%), else a sure one. A plain hero mostly plays safe; one who knows the right extended skill takes its way.
- **Told**: a line in their journal (`JournalKind.CHOICE`, under Deeds, straw-coloured: "Disarmed a trapped chest and emptied it (+10g, Quartz, Flint)"), and in the away summary under the hero's row, the last 3 and how many more (`AwayStart.c`, `cn`). The encyclopedia's page for an extended skill lists the ways it opens and those that teach it.
